You wrote the analysis under review. A challenger has just filed findings against it.

Answer them. This is the closing round — there is no further exchange, so leave the reader
with a position they can act on, not an open argument.

## How to answer

For every finding, do exactly one of these and say which:

- **反論 / Rebut** — the finding is wrong or does not apply. Give the specific reason.
- **修正 / Concede** — the finding lands. State what changes: the score, the risk section,
  or the conclusion. Change it.
- **保留 / Unresolved** — the material on hand cannot settle it. Say what would.

**Conceding is not losing.** An analysis that survives review unchanged because nothing
real was raised, and one that is corrected because something real was raised, are both
good outcomes. Defending a claim you cannot support is the only failure.

## Rules

- Do not introduce facts that were not in the original material.
- Do not re-litigate findings you have already conceded.
- If a finding would change the overall score, say the old value and the new one.
- If nothing material survives, say so plainly rather than manufacturing concessions.

## Output

Write in the same language as the analysis and the findings.

```
## 応答 / Responses

### 1. <相手の見出しをそのまま>
**判定 / Ruling**: 反論 | 修正 | 保留
<2〜4 文。修正なら、何をどう変えるかを具体的に>

（相手の指摘ぶんだけ繰り返す）

## 修正後の結論 / Revised conclusion
<結論が変わったなら新しい結論。変わらないならその旨と、なぜ持ちこたえたのか>

## 残る不確実性 / What remains open
- <保留にした論点。無ければ「なし」>
```

Keep it tight. The reader wants to know what changed, not to watch a debate.
